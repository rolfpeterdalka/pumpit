# Time simulation

Pump It can follow a network over time: tanks that fill and empty, open or closed over a gas, pumps that start and stop by level or by the clock, demands that follow a daily profile, valves that open and close, check valves, the cost of the energy of the pumps and, in the compressible mode, vessels of gas that fill and empty, heat up or change their volume. The simulation is quasi-static: a sequence of steady states, one per time step. Between two steps the tables of the junctions give their new values, events start tables, the volumes of the finite tanks and the gas of the finite vessels are integrated and the tanks close the pipes they uncover. The inertia of the fluid, pressure waves (water hammer) and the heat exchanged with walls are out of its scope.

It follows its own clean-room specification, *Simulação no tempo* (version 1.0, 2 November 2026, and version 1.1, 4 November 2026, which adds its Annex D and phase F4), written by the specification team as a complement of `Clean Room.md`. This file maps it onto Pump It and records the decisions it leaves open or that the adaptation required (its §10.7 asks for this record). In the interface the feature is called "Time simulation" ("Simulação no tempo", "Simulación en el tiempo").

Clean room (§0.3): only that specification, `Clean Room.md`, the format description of `.aro` files (version 1.1, which §0.3 of version 1.1 allows), the public references of its Annex C and the test cases of its §12 were used, with the code of this repository. No executable, help, screen, text or model file of another program was opened. The specification team took the facts of its Annex D from the structure of the program for gases, as its §0.2 records; this work used only the text of the specification. The `.fth` reader of the time data follows Annex A of the specification, as its version 1.1 corrects and completes it, and the `.aro` reader its Annex D.9; their tests write synthetic files in code ([fth-import.md](fth-import.md), F-34 to F-41; [aro-import.md](aro-import.md), A-29 to A-35).

## Decisions

The decisions of Annex B of the specification are followed as written. They are repeated here, with the place where the code applies them, together with those that the adaptation to Pump It required.

**T-01 Clock.** The time of step k is always t_k = min(t₀ + k·Δt, t_f), never a sum of steps; the number of steps is N = ⌈(t_f − t₀)/Δt − 10⁻⁹⌉, so a step that does not divide the span leaves a shorter last step that ends at t_f; N is at most 100 000 (the checklist says so). The same model gives the same results, bit for bit, in the browser and in Node: the budget of a slice of the interface only decides where a slice stops.

**T-02 Time control.** `solution.time = { on, start, stop, step, saveEvery, method, relTol, absTol, relax, maxIter, dataEnd, exactEvents, tariff }`, with the defaults of §3 (0 to 24 h, step of 10 min, the forward method, 10⁻⁵, 0.03048 mm, 1, 10 000, hold), the exact time of events off (T-29) and no tariff (T-27). It lives under the solution, so a child scenario overrides it field by field without new code (T16). With `on: false` the time data and tanks are kept and the model calculates as before. A model saved before this version has no `time` and gets the defaults when it opens (`normalizeTime`, like `normalizeRegime`).

**T-03 Bases of time.** A table started by the clock has τ = t, so a profile of 0 to 24 h simulated from 6 h starts at 6 h of the profile. A table started by an event has τ = t − t_e, where t_e is the time of the step where the event fired. The elapsed time of a condition counts from the time the current table of the junction began to apply (t₀ before any event).

**T-04 Value of a table.** Before its first point a table gives its first value; between points, a straight line or steps; two points at the same time make a step, and from that time on the second one applies. Past the last point, a repeated table starts again with the period P = τ_n − τ₁; otherwise it holds its last value (a caution, once per junction) or extrapolates the line of its last two points, limited to the domain of the quantity (a flow, a speed, a Cv, a Kv or an area is not negative, an opening stays from 0 to 100 %), with a warning once per junction. The value recorded for the table and the one a table started from the value in use looks for are the value limited, the one the junction takes. A relative table gives a percentage of the steady value of the same quantity. A valve by K is interpolated in c = 1/√K, c = 0 for a closed valve (K < 0), so that a closing ramp in K behaves like the same ramp in Cv; Cv and Kv are interpolated in their own values. The units are converted to SI once, when the run is prepared.

**T-05 What a table changes.** The quantities of each type come from its `time` hook (§4.2): the flow of an imposed flow, the source of a branch, the pressure of an imposed pressure, the level and the surface pressure of an infinite reservoir (only the surface pressure of an open finite tank, the relief pressure of a closed one, and nothing for a finite gas vessel, whose gas the simulation follows), the speed of a pump on its curve (in %), the flow of a pump in fixed flow, the set point of a pump under control and of a control valve, the pressure ratio of a compressor at a fixed ratio (T-30), the K, Cv or Kv of a valve and of an exit valve (the one of its loss model) or its opening in % (T-26), and the effective area of a nozzle. An orifice, a generic component and a check valve take no time data in this version. A nozzle with a table becomes holes of Cd = 1 with the area of the table, whatever its model; an area of 0 closes it. Nozzles and exit valves gained a `closed` field for this, honoured by both solvers.

**T-06 The first step and special conditions.** Step 0 is the model of the scenario as it is, special conditions included, for every junction, so it is the steady state exactly (T10); only a tank whose level or pressure is unknown changes it (T-23). A table by the clock applies from step 1; a table started by an event, from the step after the event. Once a table applies, the special condition of the junction no longer does: a valve is closed only if the table closes it, a pump runs while its speed (or its pressure ratio) is above zero (always, in fixed flow and under control), a control valve controls. Without time data, or with `use: false`, the special condition holds over the whole run. A table by the clock whose value at t₀ differs from the steady one (relative difference above 10⁻⁶) gives the caution of a jump between t₀ and t₁; when a table started by an event begins, a jump against the value in use goes to the event log as information.

**T-07 Triggers.** Each junction has the state machine of §5.1. Only the next event of the sequence is watched: a tank that starts full, with "start at level ≤ 10" and "stop at level ≥ 15", does not stop at the start, since it waits for the start first. An event is tested on the state of step k (its results, the levels shown at t_k and t_k itself), is logged at t_k and its table enters the solution of step k + 1, unless the exact time of events is on (T-29). Several junctions may fire in one step; all are tested on the same state.

**T-08 Conditions.** The quantities of §5.2: time, elapsed time, the level, the elevation of the surface, the volume and the surface pressure of a reservoir or tank (the pressure of the gas of a closed tank or of a gas vessel), static and stagnation pressures at the end of a pipe or the side of a junction, the grade lines at the end of a pipe, volume flow, mass flow and velocity of a pipe or a two-port junction (with the sign of its reference direction), the speed, flow and head of a pump (for a compressor of the compressible mode, its isentropic head, which the list of quantities names so), the Cv of a valve, the opening of a valve that gives one (T-26), and the state of a pump (running, stopped), a valve or a check valve (open, closed) or a control valve (controlling, open, closed). A number is compared with >, ≥, < or ≤ and a state with = or ≠. A value has a unit of the category of its quantity; a gauge pressure uses the ambient pressure of the system. A quantity without a value in a step (an isolated pipe) never holds. A condition may also compare the difference of a quantity between two objects (T-24).

**T-09 Uncovered pipes.** A connection at the elevation z_c is uncovered when z_c ≥ z_s − 10⁻⁹ m, where z_s is the surface used in the solution. A flow into the tank through it falls freely, as Pump It already did. A flow out of the tank through it is impossible: the pipe is closed and the same step is solved again (at most 50 passes; more is the error "uncovered loop"); the reference closes it only from the next step (inferred), and solving again keeps a step with an impossible flow out of the record. A closed pipe opens again when the surface used in a solution covers it (z_s > z_c + 10⁻⁹ m), and also, a decision of Pump It that the specification does not state, when the network would push liquid into the tank through it: its grade lines are compared at its two ends. Without this second rule a tank emptied through its only pipe could never fill again, since an empty tank never covers its bottom connection. Closing and opening go to the event log.

**T-10 Demand without supply.** Closing pipes, valves or pumps may leave an island with an imposed flow (an imposed flow, a source of a branch, a pump in fixed flow) and no pressure reference, which is the error "no reference" of the steady state. In the time simulation the solver gets `dropUnsupplied`: such an island is isolated with no flow, its imposed flows count as demand without supply (`res.unsupplied`, a volume in the totals, or a mass for a gas: the mass flow of the boundary, a normal volume at the normal density; a volume at the conditions of a node has no density while the node has no pressure, so its mass is not known: not available in the totals, with a caution) and the start and the end of each one go to the event log. Without the option the steady state is unchanged.

**T-11 Volume of a tank.** The state of a tank is its volume, so its mass is kept exactly; the level comes from it (a constant section, the table of volume by height, linear between its points, and its inverse, or a horizontal cylinder, T-28). Above the capacity the excess overflows and the tank is full; below zero the volume stops at zero, the shortfall counts as a deficit (the volume the network got without the tank having it), with the warning "the step is too long for the emptying of this tank" once per tank, and the tank is empty. Full, empty and back to normal go to the event log; a tank that starts empty logs "back to normal" when it begins to fill.

**T-12 Methods.** Forward (the default): step k is solved with the level at its start, and stands for [t_k, t_k+1]. Central: for k ≥ 1, step k is solved with the mean level of its interval and stands for [t_k−1, t_k]; the volume at its end is found by iteration, from the forward prediction, with the tables at t_k and the level relaxed by r. A tank has converged when |h_new − h| ≤ absTol or ≤ relTol·|h_new| (one of the two is enough, as in the reference); all tanks must converge. Without convergence in `maxIter` iterations the run stops with an error, as in the reference. The limits of a tank (full, empty) apply to every volume of the iteration, and the overflow and the deficit take only the final one. A relaxation below 0.01 gives a caution. A gas takes the forward or the implicit method (T-34); a liquid asked for the implicit one, and a gas asked for the central one, take the forward method, with a caution.

**T-13 One rule of integration.** Volumes and masses pumped and delivered, energy and its cost, the time running and the flows of the tanks and vessels use the interval each step stands for (T-12): [t_k, t_k+1] in the forward method, [t_k−1, t_k] in the central and the implicit ones. With it the volume balance of every tank closes exactly, Σ Q_net·Δt = V_final − V₀ + overflow − deficit (T12), as does the mass balance of every vessel, and the balance of the whole network is shown in the totals.

**T-14 Pumps.** A speed of 0 or less from a table stops the pump; its new field `stopMode` says how: `'closed'` (the default) blocks the flow as a check valve would, `'open'` lets it through. A start is a change from stopped to running; the time running adds the intervals of the steps where it runs. The energy adds the shaft power, or the power given to the liquid without an efficiency curve (marked "without efficiency"), and the power given to the liquid on its own. In fixed flow a flow of 0 counts as stopped. A pump under control logs when it loses its control (speed too high or too low) and when it regains it. A pump on its curve may hold a check valve (T-25).

**T-15 Control valves.** In the time simulation "always control" is taken as off in every step: a valve that cannot control takes its default action, with a caution once per valve, as in the reference.

**T-16 Warnings of the steps.** The warnings of the steady states are grouped by level, object and message, with the number of steps and the first and last time they came (§9.4). The specification asks the solver to keep the catalog key of each warning for this; Pump It's warnings carry their translated text, and the grouping uses that text with its numbers masked, which tells the messages of the catalogs apart as their keys would, without changing the solver. A step that does not converge is kept, with its critical warning in this list, and the run goes on; an error of the solver in a step stops the run, with the results up to the step before.

**T-17 Speed.** A step starts from the flows of the step before (where they are not zero) and the states of its devices (`opts.states`; a device with a special condition keeps it, its `resume`); in the compressible mode it starts from the first pressures of its own solver. A step whose model is the same as the one before (its tables, levels and closed pipes) takes the solution of the step before without solving again: this covers the step without change of §8.4 and makes T10 exact. The interface runs the simulation in slices of about 30 ms after the steady state, with its progress in the stamp; any edit cancels it and starts it again, and its results are not saved with the model.

**T-18 What is recorded.** Every recorded step (every `saveEvery`-th one, and always the last) keeps a series of every numeric field of the steady results (pipes, junctions, pumps, valves; a yes or no as 1 or 0), of the tanks (level, surface, surface pressure, volume, mass, net flows, state, and for a closed tank the volume and the pressure of its gas), of the gas vessels (pressure, temperature, mass, density, net mass flow, volume, the heat they take and their internal energy) and the value each table gave. A step at the exact time of an event (T-29) is recorded too, marked as such. Its snapshot keeps what solves the step again: the levels used, the gas pressures, the state of the vessels, the phases of the triggers, the closed pipes and the states of the devices; `snapshot(k)` gives the full steady result of the step (T11), with a cache of the last eight. Extremes, totals, warnings and the event log take every step. Before a run, the size of its series is estimated (recorded steps × fields × 8 bytes); above 200 MB a caution suggests recording fewer steps.

**T-19 Finite tanks in the model.** A reservoir gains `tank = { model: 'infinite' | 'open' | 'closed', bottom, height, shape: 'constant' | 'table' | 'horizontalCylinder', area, table: { h, V, hUnit, vUnit }, diameter, length, gas: { n, pMax, pMin }, known: { level, pressure } }`; without it, or with `'infinite'`, it is the reservoir of today. The level at the start stays in `liquidElev`, an elevation. The pipes connect as in the reservoir of today, by elevation or by depth below the surface; at t₀ a depth becomes an elevation, fixed for the run. In the steady state and in step 0 a finite tank is exactly the reservoir of today (unless its level or pressure is unknown, T-23). The checklist asks for an area above zero, a table that starts at (0, 0) with both columns rising and its last height at least that of the tank, a diameter and a length above zero, and a level between the bottom and the top; a pipe connected below the bottom gives a caution. The interface calls the field `bottom` "Bottom of the tank", apart from the elevation of the reservoir, where a pipe without an elevation of its own connects. Finite tanks are for liquids: in the compressible mode a finite tank is kept in the model and not used, and the reservoir takes its gas vessel instead (T-30).

**T-20 Interface.** The system window has the time control, with the number of steps and the memory of a run, the exact time of events (shown with the forward method only), the tariff of the energy cost and the named events of the model; the stamp "Over time" shows the progress and stops a run (what was calculated stays, marked as stopped). The time cursor (first, previous, play at a choice of speed, next, last, and a slider) picks the recorded step that the tables, the drawing, the cards and the panels show: its result is solved again from its snapshot. The inspector gives the tank of a reservoir (open or closed, with its gas, what is known at the start, a constant section, a table or a horizontal cylinder) or, in the compressible mode, its gas vessel, with the state at the start and at the cursor; the check valve, the check valve of a pump, the opening of a valve and the characteristic of a control valve. The results view gains the tabs "Over time" (every recorded step of one object, with the columns and units of its table), "Events" (by time or by object), "Extremes" and "Totals" (with the cost by pump, its total and a yearly estimate, and for a gas the masses); the summary and the warnings show the run. The chart "Over time" draws series chosen by object and quantity, one panel per unit, against seconds, minutes, hours, days or the clock. The model data list the time data, the tanks and the vessels; the `.xlsx` export adds a sheet per kind of object (time in rows, a column per object and output) and the event log. The drawing marks the junctions with time data and the finite tanks and vessels with a small clock, and the drawing of a reservoir shows the bottom and the top of its tank. With phase F4 (item 9 of D.10), the time control of a gas offers the forward and the implicit methods (a liquid, the forward and the central ones), the time data of a junction say where a table starts after its event, the table editor takes the temperature of the gas as a second column where a boundary takes it, and the inspector of a vessel gives its thermal model, its state at the start (given or calculated) and its table of heat and volume; the vessel shows its volume, the heat it takes and its internal energy.

**T-21 Units.** New unit categories: `time` (s, min, h, d), `percent`, `energy` (J, kJ, MJ, Wh, kWh, MWh) and `mass` (kg, t, lbm).

**T-22 Closed tank (§6.5).** A finite tank of model `'closed'` holds a gas over the liquid, polytropic: P_g·V_g^n = C, with absolute pressures and V_g = V_max − V. The surface pressure of the reservoir is the pressure of the gas at the start, so C₀ = P_g0·V_g0^n; the user gives n (1 is isothermal, 1.4 adiabatic air). The gas pressure is the surface pressure of the tank in the solution, at the level of the solution (with the central method, the mean level). Above the relief pressure `pMax` the gas is relieved and holds `pMax`; below the vent pressure `pMin` gas comes in and it holds `pMin`; both change C, and their opening and closing go to the event log. A tank full of liquid (V_g ≤ 10⁻⁹·V_max) with a relief keeps its level at the top at `pMax` and the excess overflows; without one, the run stops with the error "closed tank full of liquid without relief" (a decision of the specification). A table of a closed tank drives its relief pressure (§4.2). The checklist asks for n > 0, absolute pressures above zero, `pMin` below `pMax`, a gas pressure at the start between them, and room for the gas at the start (a level below the top).

**T-23 Level or pressure unknown at the start (§6.6).** `tank.known = { level, pressure }`, both known by default; the pressure of an open tank is always known, and one of the two must be known (checklist). In step 0 the tank is a free node: a branch without flow of its own at the elevation of its lowest connection, z_ref. The stagnation pressure P₀ solved there gives what is unknown: the surface z_s = z_ref + (P₀ − P_s)/(ρg), with P_s the surface pressure (the gas pressure given, for a closed tank), or the gas pressure P_s = P₀ − ρg(z_s − z_ref). A level outside the tank, or a gas pressure not above zero, stops the run at the start. Step 0 shows the network with the tank as that free node; from step 1 the tank behaves normally, and its totals start from the volume found.

**T-24 Combined events and differences (§5.4, §5.2).** The named events live in `model.events`, the same in every scenario, and `effective()` copies them. A named event is a condition (`cond`) or an operator (`and`, `or`, `not`) over the names of other events; `not` holds when none of its events holds. A junction uses one with the trigger `combined`, which, like a single event, starts its table once. The checklist refuses an empty or repeated name, a missing reference, an operator with too few events (two for `and` and `or`, one for `not`) and a cycle, and a junction may use only a complete event. Renaming an event in the system window carries the new name to the events and the junctions that use it. A condition may compare the difference of a quantity between two objects (`obj2`, `end2`) with its value: the difference of two pressures takes a unit of pressure difference, never a gauge one. A state has no difference.

**T-25 Check valves (§7.4).** The junction type `check` comes back, as the specification asks: a model that holds one opens with it, the fields it lacks filled in, closed when its special condition closed it (the conversion into a valve is gone). Open, its loss is a K, a Cv or a Kv; it closes when the velocity in its reference direction falls below its closing velocity (0 by default) and opens again when the pressure difference across it reaches its reopening difference (0 by default). A pump on its curve gains the option `noReverse` (off by default, so existing models do not change): its check valve closes when the flow would go back and opens again when the pressure the pump gives at no flow (its shutoff head) overcomes the pressure against it. Both are devices of the solver, so they close and open within the same step, which is solved again, and their state passes to the next step (hysteresis); a separate check valve of the reference reacts only in the next step, and the specification decides against that delay. Small margins (10⁻⁹ m/s and 10⁻³ Pa) keep them from chattering on a value that is zero but for rounding. A pump driven backwards on its curve may keep the solution from converging, so the solver also reviews these devices (`device.anyway`) after a solution that did not converge, and solves again when one of them changes. Their closing and opening go to the event log, and a condition can test the state of a check valve. They belong to the solver of liquids: in the compressible mode the check valve is refused as the other types without a gas model, and the pump does not offer the option.

**T-26 Opening of a valve (§7.2).** A valve and an exit valve gain the loss model "opening": the Cv fully open `CvMax`, a characteristic, linear (Cv = Cv_max·x) or of equal percentage (Cv = Cv_max·R^(x−1), with the rangeability R above 1), and the opening `open` in % (x = open/100). An opening of 0 closes the valve, also with equal percentage. A table of such a valve drives its opening. A control valve may take a characteristic too, optional: its opening in the results is then 0 % closed, 100 % fully open and, while it controls, the inverse of the characteristic at its Cv. A condition can compare the opening of a valve that gives one. In the compressible mode the Cv of an opening gives the loss of the gas as a Cv does.

**T-27 Energy cost (§9.5).** `solution.time.tariff = { use, currency, t, v, tUnit }`: the price per kWh in steps by the hour of the day, from 0 h, repeated every day (t mod 24 h). The currency is only a label. The cost of a step is its energy (the power times the interval it stands for, T-13) times the price at the time of the step. The power is the shaft power, since Pump It has no data of the motor; without an efficiency curve it is the power given to the liquid (the efficiency of 100 % the pump assumes, `etaAssumed`), marked "without efficiency" in the energy, in the cost of the pump, in the total and in the extremes of its power; with an efficiency curve that gives no usable value at a step the pump runs (outside 0 to 100 %, or no speed of its control), the energy and the cost are not known. A compressor gives its energy to the gas: its total is labelled as such, without a column of energy given to the liquid, and its extremes are of the power given to the gas and of the isentropic head. The totals give the cost of each pump and of the network, the time the run integrated (the sum of the intervals its steps stood for, T-13, so a run stopped before its end counts only what it calculated) and, when that time is a whole number of days D, a yearly estimate, cost × 365/D, labelled as an estimate; otherwise the card says there is none. The checklist asks for a first time of 0, rising times within the day and prices not below zero.

**T-28 Horizontal cylinder (§6.2).** A tank may be a horizontal cylinder of diameter D = 2R and length L, whose bottom is its lowest point and whose height is its diameter. Its volume at the level h is that of the circular segment, V = L·[R²·acos((R − h)/R) − (R − h)·√(2Rh − h²)], and the level of a volume is found by bisection to 10⁻¹² m.

**T-29 Exact time of an event (§5.3, optional).** The option `exactEvents` (off by default, so the rule of T-07 holds) applies to the forward method and to the events on the level, the surface elevation or the volume of a finite tank, without a difference; the interface shows it with the forward method only, and the central method ignores it. After a step the volumes at the end of its interval are known (the forward integration). When an armed condition, false at t_k, holds at t_k+1 on a straight line between the two values, the step is cut at the crossing t*. The value at the end is that of the volume limited to the tank, so the line ends where the tank fills or empties when that comes before t_k+1, not at t_k+1: the volumes are integrated up to t*, the event fires at t* and a step is solved there with the new table, recorded and marked, before the clock goes on to t_k+1. The level of a constant section is a straight line in time, so its crossing is exact; with a table or a cylinder it is the straight line of the specification. With several crossings in one interval the earliest one is taken, and the rest of the interval is checked again from it.

**T-30 Gas vessels (§11).** A reservoir of the compressible mode gains `vessel = { model: 'infinite' | 'finite', volume }`; infinite, it is the gas reservoir of today. A finite vessel holds a fixed volume V of an ideal gas with the Z of the system; its state is its mass M and its internal energy U = M·c_v·T. At the start its pressure is the surface pressure of the reservoir, and its temperature that of the gas it supplies in the adiabatic model, or that of the system in the isothermal one (the vessel follows the model of the system, `gasMode`). At every step the vessel is the gas reservoir of today, a node at the stagnation pressure and the temperature of its gas, and the solver of the compressible mode solves the network. After the step, forward: M′ = M + Δt·(Σṁ_in − Σṁ_out); isothermal, the temperature stays; adiabatic, U′ = U + Δt·c_p·(Σṁ_in·T₀,in − ṁ_out·T) and T = U′/(M′·c_v); then P = M′·Z·R·T/V. The gas that leaves takes the temperature of the vessel, the gas that comes in brings the stagnation temperature of its pipe at the vessel, and the walls exchange no heat (a decision of the specification). A vessel that would empty within one step stops the run, with an error that asks for a shorter step.

Version 1.0 of the specification gave only the forward update; version 1.1 adds the implicit one (T-34), and a vessel may take a thermal model of its own (T-32). The central method is still not used for a gas: a gas run asked for it takes the forward method, with a caution. A finite liquid tank kept on a reservoir is not used for a gas; a finite vessel takes no table, and an infinite one keeps the tables of its level and pressure. The time data of a gas are those of the junctions that have a gas model (§4.2), with the pressure ratio of a compressor at a fixed ratio, where 0 or less stops it as a speed does. A flow of a gas over time is a mass flow or a normal volume flow (Nm³), converted with the normal density: a volume at the conditions of a pipe changes with them, so the checklist refuses it in a table and in a junction with time data, and the table editor does not offer it. The results of a vessel are its pressure, temperature, mass, density and net mass flow at every step, the extremes of its pressure and temperature, and its mass balance in the totals (in, out, change and closure); the other totals of a gas are masses.

**T-31 The solver of the compressible mode.** The cases of §11 join a vessel to a short, wide pipe, a link of almost no loss, and needed two changes of the solver of the compressible mode. A Newton step below the resolution of the unknowns (32 units in the last place) with every misfit under 10⁻⁶ ends the iteration as converged, since nothing can balance such a node more finely. The slopes of a link whose drive is too small for the finite differences of its march (below 10⁻⁶ of its pressure, or with no usable difference) are those of a quadratic loss, dG/dΔP = G/(2·ΔP). Both act only where the solver could not resolve before, and the cases of `tests/gas.test.cjs` keep their results; [gas-decisions.md](gas-decisions.md) records them (G-37).

**T-32 Thermal model of a vessel (item 1 of D.10).** A finite vessel gains `thermal: 'system' | 'adiabatic' | 'isothermal' | 'heatVolume'`, `'system'` by default, which follows the model of the system as before. With `'heatVolume'` a table by the clock (`vessel.time`, the structure of the time data with two columns: the heat rate Q̇ into the gas and the volume V above zero) drives the vessel, and its energy balance becomes U′ = U + Δt·(c_p·(Σṁ_in·T₀,in − ṁ_out·T) + Q̇) − P*·(V′ − V), then T = U′/(M′·c_v) and P = M′·Z·R·T/V′. The work of the boundary −P*·ΔV is a decision of the specification (the reference does not document it). With the forward method T and P* are those of the start of the step, and Q̇ that of the time of the step; with the implicit one, those of its end. The table is read at the time of each step from the start of the simulation, the first step included: the heat and the volume of a vessel do not change the network, so the first step is still the steady state (T21 needs the heat of the first interval). The table is always absolute, since the steady state has no heat; its volume at the start is the volume of the vessel, and a table that starts at another volume than the field gives a caution, as rule 1 of D.3. With `'adiabatic'` and `'isothermal'` the volume is fixed and there is no heat. A vessel isothermal or adiabatic of its own works in a system of either model: the network keeps the model of the system.

**T-33 State of a vessel at the start (item 2 of D.10).** `vessel.known`, true by default. False, the first step solves the network with the vessel as a free node without a flow of its own, as a tank of §6.6, and its pressure and temperature of stagnation there are those of its gas at the start, which give its mass and its energy. A vessel cut off from the network, whose node has no pressure, stops the run at the start.

**T-34 The implicit method (item 4 of D.10).** `solution.time.method` takes `'implicit'`, a method of the vessels of a gas. Step k is solved with each vessel at the state of the end of its interval [t_k−1, t_k], so the flows of that solution are those of the balance, with the tables of t_k. The state is found by Newton from the forward prediction: for each vessel, the misfit between the pressure and temperature tried and those its balance gives with the flows of the solution; its derivatives by differences, solving the network again with its pressure moved by 10⁻⁵ of its value and with its temperature moved the same (only the pressure for an isothermal vessel). That is one Newton per vessel, with its own derivatives, as the specification describes the reference; its step is multiplied by the relaxation and goes at most half way to zero. The method has converged when every misfit is within `relTol` of its value, and the vessel then takes the state of its balance, so its mass balance closes exactly. Without convergence in `maxIter` iterations the run stops, as with the central method. A step costs three solutions of the network for an isothermal vessel and four for another one when Newton converges in one iteration, which is the usual case. The integration of the totals takes the interval [t_k−1, t_k] (T-13). The exact time of events stays an option of the forward method.

**T-35 A table that starts from the value in use (item 5 of D.10).** `TimeData.resume: 'start' | 'current'`, `'start'` by default. With `'current'`, when an event puts a table in use, the table starts at the first time τ*, from its first point on, at which it gives the value the junction uses at that step: at a point, or on the straight line between two points of a linear table, compared in the space of the table (1/√K for a valve by K) and in SI (a relative table is in SI after its preparation, so the comparison is that of the percentages), within 10⁻⁹ of the value; an infinite value (K = 0) is only the same as another infinite one. A table that never gives it starts at its first point, τ* = 0. Then t_e = t_k − τ*, and the elapsed time still counts from t_k. It does not apply to a table by the clock. The value in use at the start is that of the model with its special condition: a valve closed by it uses the value that closes it (K = −1, a Cv, a Kv or an opening of 0), a stopped pump a speed, a ratio or a flow of 0, a closed nozzle an area of 0; the hooks of the types give it (`stopped`). The log gives τ* when it is above zero, and the jump from the value in use to the value where the table starts, as before. The importers write `'current'`, the behaviour of the reference (T23 checks both).

**T-36 Rules of coherence (D.3, item 8 of D.10).** The checklist gains, as errors, the rules of D.3 where Pump It has the matching feature, in both regimes: 2, a relative table starts at 100 %, in each of its columns, for the first table (a second table takes over from the first one, not from the steady state); 4, a repeated table ends with the values it starts with; 5, no relative table on a junction its special condition closes or stops; 6, a junction its special condition closes or stops (a valve, an exit valve, a nozzle, a control valve closed, a pump stopped) starts its first table at the value that closes or stops it, which a set point never is; 9, a pump takes only the special conditions that fit its table: a speed or a pressure ratio, running or stopped with free passage, a flow or a set flow, running or stopped without flow, a set pressure, running; 7, an opening between 0 and 100 %; 8, a quantity with a steady value (a table of the relief pressure of a closed tank that has none); 12, a difference across a junction with two pipes or more. Rule 1 stays a caution (§4.5). Rule 3 needs nothing: Pump It converts the units of a table, and the checklist already asks for a unit of the category of the quantity; rules 11 and 13 were already there. Rule 10, a control valve that always controls, stays a caution in both regimes, a decision against the letter of item 8: the time simulation already takes "always control" as off in every step (T-15), so refusing the run would add nothing, and the option is on by default in every new control valve. Models of the earlier versions whose tables contradict these rules now show them in the checklist.

**T-37 Temperature of the gas over time (item 3 of D.10).** A table of an imposed pressure, of an imposed flow that enters and of an infinite reservoir (with its surface pressure) may have a second column, the temperature of the gas the boundary supplies (`v2`, `v2Unit` in each table), in the adiabatic model of the system; the isothermal model has one temperature, and the hooks offer no second column there. Without it the steady temperature applies: the temperature of the junction, or that of the sources. In a relative table it is a percentage of the absolute steady temperature (a decision of the specification). The checklist asks for a value at each time, in a unit of temperature, above absolute zero (in a relative table, the percentage of the steady temperature), and the rules of T-36 apply to it as to the first column. The table editor shows the column when the option is on.

**T-38 Limits of the solver of the compressible mode met on the way.** Two limits of the steady solver of a gas, older than this phase, showed with its cases. They were not changed here, since that would change the results of gas models. (1) In the isothermal model a junction is a node of static pressure and a reservoir one of stagnation pressure (G-07 of [gas-decisions.md](gas-decisions.md)). Through a pipe whose losses are below one velocity head, as the short wide pipe of T20, the gas reaches the reservoir with a stagnation pressure above the static pressure it left with, so an imposed flow into a reservoir has no solution and the solver reports a singular system. T20 runs with the short pipe in the adiabatic model, its vessel isothermal (T-32), and in the isothermal model with a pipe 10 m long; with an imposed flow the vessel does not depend on the pipe. (2) A small choked nozzle, a few millimetres, at the end of a wide pipe may not converge in the adiabatic model; the run keeps such a step with its critical warning (T-16).

## Inferred by the specification

The specification marks these points as inferred from examples and files of the reference, not from its documentation. Pump It follows them as written:

- an event is logged at the step where its condition is seen and takes effect from the next step (§5.3);
- the reference closes an uncovered pipe only from the next step (§6.4; Pump It closes it in the same step, T-09);
- in `.fth` files: `Frequency` is the recording interval, `DifferenceMethod` 0 is the forward method and 1 the central one, and model 2 of a tank is a closed tank (Annex A; [fth-import.md](fth-import.md)). Version 1.1 of the specification (4 November 2026) confirmed trigger 3, two events in sequence, and corrected two other readings of version 1.0: comparison 2 is "equal to", not "<", and whether a table holds relative values is a field of the file, not a table in "Percent" of a quantity that is not a percentage (F-35 to F-39 of [fth-import.md](fth-import.md));
- of a gas vessel (Annex D.6): the gas that leaves takes the enthalpy of the vessel, and the state at the start that the reference calculates comes from the solution of the first step (T-33).
