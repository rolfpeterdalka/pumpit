# Import of .imp files

*File → Open* (or a file dropped on the window) reads a `.imp` model, a model of hydraulic transients of a liquid network, and turns one of its scenarios into a Pump It model. The reader is `src/core/import/imp.js` (`PumpIt.importers.imp`). It reads the network of the liquid with the reader it shares with the `.fth` importer, `src/core/import/liquid.js` (`PumpIt.importLiquid`), the blocks of the kinds of junction item by item with `src/core/import/items.js` (`PumpIt.importItems`), and the data of the water hammer with `src/core/import/imp-surge.js` (`PumpIt.importImpSurge`), which writes them into the fields of the module of the water hammer ([water-hammer.md](water-hammer.md)); what the whole family of formats shares is `src/core/import/shared.js`. The choice of the scenario and the report are those of `.fth` files (`src/ui/app/import-report.js`). The checks are `tests/imp-import.test.cjs`, and `tests/validate-imp.js` checks the import on the user's own files (I-43).

## Clean room

**Sources.** The importer follows only the description of the format written by the specification team (`formato-imp.md`, version 1 of 23 November 2026), the answers of that team to the questions of phases I2 and I3 (`formato-imp-respostas.md`, 24 November 2026) and the answers of that team to the questions on them (`formato-imp-respostas-2.md`, 24 November 2026), which correct parts of the description, and the documents the description refers to: the description of the `.fth` format of 20 October 2026 (`formato-fth.md`), Annexes A.2 and D of the specification of the time simulation (version 1.1 of 4 November 2026), the specification of the water hammer (version 1.0 of 5 November 2026) with the addendum of its specification team for phase G3 (16 November 2026), `Clean Room.md`, and this repository. The specification team surveyed the format on data files and took from the program only facts (the order in which it writes the fields of each record, the values of its enumerations, some comparisons and conversions of its routines), as section 3 of the description and the introduction of the answers record; this work used only the text of those documents.

- No `.imp`, `.fth` or `.aro` file was opened during this work, and nothing of the program, of its help or of the working folder of the specification team was used. The repository holds no `.imp` file (`*.imp` is in `.gitignore`).
- Where the description did not say enough, this work took a reading, gave the user a note, and sent the question to the specification team in writing (`formato-imp-perguntas.md`, 24 November 2026); the answers came back the same way and replaced those readings. No answer was taken from a file. The two questions the answers left open (`formato-imp-perguntas-2.md`) were answered the same way (`formato-imp-respostas-2.md`, 24 November 2026), and the readings they gave replaced those of this work; no question is open.
- The tests write their files in code from the items of Annex A of the description.
- The product names no other program: the menu says ".imp", the notes speak of "the program it came from", and the first line of a file is recognised by a hash of its program word (I-01). The names of the items are those of the description, which it gives as function, not content.
- Curves of a library of the program (four quadrants, deceleration of check valves, opening profiles) are not in the files and do not come into Pump It (W-51 of [water-hammer.md](water-hammer.md)).

## Decisions of the description

The decisions of section 20.1 of the description are followed as written, I-13 and I-24 to I-32 with the corrections of the answers of its team (`formato-imp-respostas.md`, 24 November 2026; "answer n" below is its section n). They are repeated here with the place where the code applies them.

**I-01 Importer and first line.** `src/core/import/imp.js` registers `PumpIt.importers.imp`, and `PROGRAMS.transient` in `shared.js` is the hash 0x1cd8c174 of the program word of the first line. A model of the `.fth` or of the `.aro` format is refused with a message that names the extension to give it, and a model of another program of the family is refused. A file without the first line of a model is read when it has a network, as a `.fth` file is (F-02). The `.fth` importer still refuses a transient model (F-02). The menu and the file input offer `.imp` because `files.js` lists the extensions of `PumpIt.importers`.

**I-02 One reader of the network of a liquid.** The reading of the network that was inside `read` of `fth.js` is `liquid.js`, shared by both importers. A format gives it what is its own (`fmt`): how its first line is checked and the message of a file that is not a model; the names of its kinds of junction for the notes; the places of the counted lists and the merge of the junction records of child scenarios; whether the time simulation of `.fth` files applies; the tail of a pump configuration; the codes of the loss models; where the control by speed and the suction of a pump on a single pipe are; the readers of the kinds it completes; where the time data of a junction are; and hooks that receive each pipe and each junction read and the whole model at the end. `fth.js` gives the reader of `.fth` files as it was. Its output did not change: every import made by `tests/fth-import.test.cjs` and `tests/aro-import.test.cjs` (169 of them) was recorded before the change and compared after it, model, notes and stored results, and both files of tests pass unchanged. The table of the kinds of junction of `.fth` files stays in `fth.js`, where its tests read it.

**I-03 Pump configurations.** A configuration has 15 fields after the list of the coefficients of its efficiency in `.imp` files, 13 in `.fth` files: `pumpConfigs(record, t, tail)` in `shared.js`. So the relative speed and the control of a pump are read where they are, also when the speed is not 100 %.

**I-04 Check valve (10).** Its items `lossType` and `loss` give the loss of a check valve of Pump It: 0 a Cv, 1 a K, 7 a Kv. The special condition 1 closes it (`closed: true`). A loss curve, which the description does not list for a check valve, becomes a K as for an exit valve (F-21).

**I-05 Generic component (20).** The item `loss`, at t+11+nc, holds a K (code 1), a Cv (0) or a Kv (7); a loss curve (3) is read as in `.fth` files (F-21). Pump It gives a generic component a K, so a Cv or a Kv becomes the K of the same loss (I-39).

**I-06 Nozzle (16).** Model 0 is a discharge coefficient with the flow area of the holes: `sprayModel: 'CdA'`, with `Cd` from the item `loss`, the number of holes from `holes` (−1 is one) and the diameter of each hole √(4·A/(π·n)) from the total area `fa`, so that the effective area Cd·A is that of the file. Models 1 (a K factor) and 3 (a loss curve) are read as in `.fth` files (F-24). The fields of each pipe come first in the block, so the other fields move with the number of pipes; the reader of `.fth` files takes the places of a nozzle on one pipe. Model 0 is a probable reading of the description, with a note.

**I-07 Kv.** The loss code 7 is a Kv, in valves, exit valves, check valves and generic components (`lossCodes` of the format). The `.fth` importer keeps its codes 0 (Cv) and 1 (K).

**I-08 A record one field short.** A record may end one field before its last item; that item is empty. A record that ends elsewhere has a note (I-38).

**I-09 Blocks read and merged by items.** `items.js` holds the items of Annex A for kinds 1 to 12, 16, 17, 20, 21, 22, 24 and 25, and those of a pump configuration. A block is walked from its start, each item taking the size its own counts give: a field, a counted list, a table [key, nY, n, unit of x, nY units of y, n rows], an event of 8 fields, the fields of each pipe, a group, the configurations of a pump, and the time data. Every named item is then found by its place, whatever the lists and tables before it hold. The record of a child scenario is walked with the same items and its own counts, and merged with its parent item by item:

- a field: empty inherits, filled replaces;
- a counted list: n a new list, −n the list of the parent with the filled values of the child, 0 the list of the parent (F-07);
- a table: its key as a field and nY as a count, its units as fields, its rows as a list, row by row, each empty value inheriting the value of the parent at its row and column;
- a group and the configurations of a pump: as lists of structures, element by element, each configuration with its own lists;
- the fields of each pipe follow their pipe when the child changes the list of pipes, as in `.fth` files (F-08).

The merged record is whole, so the readers of the kinds read it as a record of the base scenario. The kinds without a schema (13, 14, 15, 18, 19, 23 and 26 to 29) merge as in `.fth` files (`mergeJunction` of `shared.js`).

The data of the water hammer (phases I2 and I3) are read by `imp-surge.js` from the hooks of the reader of the network: `start` before the pipes, `pipe` for each pipe, the readers of the kinds of the protection devices, the hook `junction` of `imp.js` for each junction and `finish` at the end. They are read only while the module of the water hammer is in the page (`PumpIt.surge`, W-01): without it the hooks return at once, nothing of the water hammer comes in, and a note says so.

**I-10 Transient block of a pipe.** Its anchor w is the first field from b+60 that is a unit of velocity, a length per second, minute, hour or day (`anchorOf`). In a child scenario the block starts 9 fields before its place in the whole record of the parent: w(child) = b(child) + (w(parent) − b(parent)) − 9 (`mergeSurgePipe`, given to `scenarioRecords` of `shared.js` as `merge.pipe`). The units at w, w−4, w−6 and w−9 of the child are checked first (I-44); a block that does not check is left out, with a note. Otherwise, from w−10 to the end of the record, an empty field inherits and a filled one replaces. The fields up to b+59 merge as in `.fth` files (`mergePipe`).

**I-11 Wave speed given.** `pipe.surge` takes `wave: 'given'` and `a`, the value at w−1 in its unit (m/s, or ft/s for a length in feet, inches or miles), and keeps for a speed calculated by Pump It the wall (w−7, w−6), the modulus of elasticity (w−5 in the unit of pressure at w−4, read as a modulus), Poisson's ratio (w−3, when it lies above 0 and below 0.5), the support and c₁. A bulk modulus of the pipe itself (w−10 above 0) gets a note only: the wave speed of the file takes it already. A pipe partly full (w−8 other than −1) is taken as full, with a note. Without a wave speed (w−1 not above 0), Pump It calculates it from the wall, with a note.

**I-12 Supports.** The codes 0 to 10 of w+3 are `user`, `thinUp`, `thinAnchored`, `thinJoints`, `thickUp`, `thickAnchored`, `thickJoints`, `tunnelUnlined`, `tunnelLined`, `thinAnchored` (9, buried by the stiffness of the soil, which Pump It does not have; a note) and `buried`. The stiffness of the rock of supports 7 and 8 is read eight fields after the name of the ambient fluid, the first text after w+20 that is not a unit, with its unit of pressure; the increase of support 10 is the last field of the record. Both are structure only, with a note.

**I-13 Stations.** w+5: 0 `'all'`, 1 `'ends'`, 5 the list of w+7 to w+11: station numbers, 0 at the inlet of the pipe and N, the number of its sections in the file (w+2), at the outlet, −1 an empty place (answer 2). With the codes 0 and 1 the five places may keep what a list chosen before left in them, and they are not read. Another code keeps `'default'`, with a note. When Pump It sections the pipe otherwise, the stations move to the same places (I-46). The control keeps `stations: 'ends'`.

**I-14 Time step.** `dtMode: 'given'` with `dt` = `TimeStep`, in seconds; `varianceMax` from `MaxErrorOption` (0 is 5 %, 1 is 10 %, 3 the value of `MaxError`), `ncMin` and `ncMax` from `MinSections` and `MaxSections`. `TimeStep` 0 takes the automatic search, with a note. Three checks give notes: the sections Pump It gives each pipe with that step (`PumpIt.surge.sectioning.section`) against w+2, `TotalSteps` against the duration over the time step (beyond one step), and the impedance a/A of w+1 (psi per lbm/s) against the wave speed and the area of the pipe (beyond 0.1 %).

**I-15 Duration and start.** `duration` = `StopTime` − `StartTime`, in `TimeUnits`. A start other than 0 is subtracted from the tables by the clock (cut at the start, I-47) and from the events on the time (code 0), with a note; a table started by an event and the time elapsed since an event (code 23) count from the event and do not move.

**I-16 to I-19 The keys of the control.** `VariableResistance` 0, −1 and 1 are `friction` `'steady'`, `'variable'` and `'brunone'`; `ModelCavitation` 0, 1 and 2 are `cavitation` `'none'`, `'dvcm'` and `'dgcm'`; `Psi`, `VoidFraction` and `VoidFractionPressure` with its unit are `psi`, `alpha0` and `p0`; `CavitationPressureType` 0 and 1 are `cavBasis` `'stagnation'` and `'static'`; `StopOnArtificialTransient` (−1 stops), `Criteria` and `CriteriaMinFlow` with its unit are `artificial.stop`, `criterion` and `minFlow`; `Frequency` is `saveEvery`. A code that is not known keeps the default of Pump It, with a note. The keys are read in any case, along the chain of scenarios (`keysOf`).

**I-20 The water hammer on.** `solution.surge` is written with `on: true` when `AnalysisType` (along the chain) is 3 and `StopTime` is above `StartTime`, and the time simulation is off. A `StopTime` of −1 writes the control off, with a note; another type of analysis too.

**I-21 Time data in j.surge.** `surgeData` reads the block as `readTimeData` of `liquid.js` reads `j.time` from a `.fth` file, with the readers of `timeData` of `shared.js` (events, tables, options) and the quantities the type takes in the water hammer (`PumpIt.surge.inputs.quantities`, given to `tableQuantity` as its list). The unit None on a valve is the value of its loss model (K, Cv or Kv), whose quantity has no unit. A junction whose type takes no table in the water hammer (a nozzle, a closed tank) has its data left out, with a note. The objects of the events are checked in `j.surge` as in `j.time` (`checkObjects(m, 'surge')`). `j.time` stays empty (W-14).

**I-22 The flag ign.** 2 on an imposed flow or pressure of one pipe is `surgeSpecial: 'infinitePipe'`, its tables not read (W-45); on a junction that Pump It reads as a node of several pipes, a note. 1 keeps the data, not in use. −1 (does not apply) is 0. The reference pressure of an infinite pipe (`useRef` of an imposed flow) has a note.

**I-23 Time data of a surge tank.** The block of a surge tank keeps its two events and its two tables before the fields of its check valve, and `rel`, `ign`, `rep1`, `rep2` and `trig` after them: the items of Annex A read them in that order. They give the surface pressure over time, which a surge tank of Pump It does not have (open to the atmosphere): a note.

**I-24 Models of a pump.** `tModel` 0 is no data: tables over time (the set point of a control) are left out with a note, since the water hammer freezes the speed of a control (W-13). 1 is a table of the speed (`what: 'speed'`); 2 a trip; 3 a trip with the four-quadrant data (I-25); 4 a start with a known speed, a table of the speed from 0 % with the four-quadrant data, left out with a note without them; 5 a start (W-50); 6 a start with the four-quadrant data; 7 the four-quadrant data alone. The inertia (`kg-m2`, `lbm-ft2`, `lbm-in2`), the rated speed (rpm) and the check valve of the pump (−1 is `noReverse`) are read; a closing velocity or a reopening difference of that check valve (its `dpType` as that of a check valve, I-27), which the check valve of a pump of Pump It does not have, gets a note. A trip (2, 3) or a start (4 to 6) happens only at the events of its trigger, never by the clock, and of two events the second makes the manoeuvre back, which Pump It does not have (answer 1, I-45). `startSpeedType` is the axis of the speeds of the table of the motor (I-26), not the final speed of a start. A sinusoidal speed has a note (I-36).

**I-25 Four quadrants from the user only.** `fqSource` 0 is the table `T:fqData` of the user, into `j.fourQuad` (`use`, `ref`, `theta`, `wh`, `wb`). It has `fqIncr` + 1 rows from 0° to 360° in equal steps of 360°/`fqIncr`, the last equal to the first, and the program reads WH and WB by the order of the rows, not their column of θ: it uses the first `fqIncr` rows and comes back to the first after the last, the curves being periodic (answer 9.2). So θ goes by the row, k·360°/`fqIncr` from 0, whatever the column holds (in a valid table they agree), and the table closes at 360° on its first point (G3-01); a table with fewer rows than `fqIncr` is left out, with a note. `fqAngleType` is the definition of θ, always in degrees (answer 9.1): 0 θ = π + atan2(v, α), that of Pump It; 1 θ = atan2(α, v) from 0 to 360°, moved to that of Pump It by θ_P = (270° − θ) mod 360°, WH and WB as they are, then sorted, with a note (α = v = 1 is 45° in the file and 225° in Pump It). When the points moved do not hold 0° (a number of increments that is not a multiple of 4), the point of 0° and 360° is the one on the straight line between the last point and the first, the linear interpolation Pump It makes between them anyway. Another code of `fqAngleType` leaves the table out, with a note. `fqRated` 0 is the reference of the steady state, 1 the best efficiency. `fqSource` 1 to 26, a curve of the library of the program, is missing data, with a note (W-51); −1 is none. The program interpolates its tables by three points; Pump It in straight lines (G3-01), which the answer gives as information only.

**I-26 Torque of the motor of a start.** In the starts by the motor (`tModel` 5 and 6) the first table is always the torque of the motor against the speed, its values in a unit of torque (`N-m`, `lbf-ft`, `lbf-in`, `kg-m`); a start with a known speed (4) has a table of its speed instead. The axis of the speeds is that of `startSpeedType` (answer 3): 0 the speed of the shaft in rpm (x in `RPM`), 1 % of the rated speed of the pump (the item `rpm`), 2 % of the synchronous speed of the motor, `syncRpm` (−1 with the types 0 and 1). They become % of the rated speed, the axis of `j.motor`: 100·x/`rpm`, x, and x·`syncRpm`/`rpm` (I-49). The same table restarts a pump after a trip of two events, which Pump It does not make (I-45). Without a table with a unit of torque, a note asks for it.

**I-27 Check valves.** `vClose` is read from `vClose` and `vCloseU` (a velocity). The reopening difference `dpOpen` is in the form `dpType` gives (answer 4.1): 0 a difference of head of the liquid in a unit of length, ΔP = ρ·g·Δh with the density of the file, given in kPa (psi for a length in feet, inches or miles); 1 a pressure difference, in any unit of pressure (`reopening`); another code keeps the default of Pump It, with a note. The dpOpen of a disc (models 2 and 3) is the value the program works out from its weight, spring and area, not a datum, and a disc of Pump It opens again by its own torque: it is not read. The tables of opening and of closing over time have a note. `model` 0 is the instantaneous valve; 1, 2 and 3 are `dynamic` `'decel'`, `'swing'` and `'nozzle'` (`decelOf`, `swingOf` and `slidingOf` in `imp-surge.js`):

- by deceleration, only with the table of the user (`perfType` 2): `T:decelTable`, the deceleration (a length per second or minute squared) against the reverse velocity, into `j.decel`; its window `decelWindow` in seconds, −1 the method of the average, which Pump It does not have (I-58). `perfType` 0 or 1 takes a curve of the library (`dynSource`): the valve stays instantaneous, with a note (W-51);
- a swinging disc, whose torques equal those of the program with θ_P = `thMax` − θ from the seat, θ_max = `thMax` − `thMin` and the seat inclined by γ_c = 90° − `thMax` (I-57): `I` with the inertias of the counterweight and of the liquid it drags (I-54), `rcg` (also the arm of the pressure, confirmed by answer 7), `Ad`, the weight `Wd` as the program takes it, without buoyancy, as its mass m = Wd/g with no fluid mass (I-54), the spring per degree `ks` and its torque at the seat `Ts0`, only with a spring (I-55; the second answers), `T:cvTheta` from the seat (I-56), the counterweight and the position of the valve (I-57);
- a sliding disc: `AdT`, `xMax`, the spring `ksT` and its force at the seat `Fs0`, `T:cvTravel` against the travel from the seat, `xMax` less the travel of the program (I-56), its weight `WdT` without buoyancy and the mass of liquid it moves (I-54), its position (I-57).

The readings of the swinging and sliding discs, probable in the answers, have a note that asks to confirm them.

**I-28 Relief valve (21).** `valveType` 2 (at an outlet) and 3 (in line at an outlet) are a `reliefValve`; 1, internal between two pipes, a closed valve between them, with a note, since Pump It does not discharge from a pipe into another. `crack` is `Pset`, `over` the full opening pressure `Pfull`, and `blowdown` the closing pressure `Pclose`, all three on the base `crackType` and `balanced` give, never relative to `crack` (answer 10.1, I-59); `back` is `Pext` (0 or None the ambient pressure). The loss: 0 a Cv, 7 a Kv, 1 a K as `CdA` = A/√K at the area of the inlet pipe, 5 a Cv with a note (still not decoded; a hypothesis). `openType` 0 is `mode: 'instant'`, 2 `'pressure'`, 1 `'pressure'` with a note. `closeType` 2 takes `Pclose`; 3 (never) and 1 (over time) have a note. `rate` is the smallest speed applied, as for a control valve (I-32). The other options (`preset`, `remoteUse`, `anticip`, `cdaSource`, `api526`) and the profiles over time have a note. A relief valve closed by its special condition is a node of its pipes, with a note, as every device.

**I-29 Gas accumulator (22).** An `airVessel` with `n`; `gasType` 0 is `gasInit: 'given'` with `Vg0` = `V`; another value a pre-charge, `Ppre` = `gasP` for `Vpre` = `V` (a hypothesis, with a note; its temperature is not used, W-21). `Vmax` and `Vmin` in `VlimU` (−1 none) are `vol` and `vgMin`. `geometry` 0 is `shape: 'none'`; another value `'table'` from `T:geoTable` in `geoHU` and `geoVU`, the top its `height` or the last height of the table, or a note when the table does not read. The orifice, when `restrictor` is other than 0, and the riser are those of I-53; the relief of its gas has a note.

**I-30 Surge tank (24).** A `surgeTank` with the K of each pipe (`P3`: the K into the pipe and the K into the tank, the order of the other kinds; a difference of elevation of a connection has a note), its top (`height`, 0 none), a constant section (`area`) or, with `useVolTable`, the table of volume by height (its top its last height when the file gives none), the orifice, when `restrictor` is other than 0, and the riser of I-53, and `oneWay` (the data of its check valve, whose `dpType` is that of a check valve, with a note). `useRef` other than 0 is `level: 'given'` at `refLevel` (a hypothesis, with a note); 0 the level of the steady state. A surface pressure other than the ambient one, a dip pipe, the gas of a closed tank and the tables over time (I-23) have a note.

**I-31 Air valve (25).** An `airValve`: `avType` 0 to 3 are `'both'`, `'in'`, `'out'` and `'twoStage'`; `CdAin`, `CdAout` and `CdAmid` (as `CdAout2`) in their units of area; `crack` as `Popen`, a pressure or a piezometric head by `crackType`; the air at the temperature of the liquid by `liqTType`, as `Tair`; `gasType` 0 air and 1 another gas; the switch of the intermediate stage by `trigType`, 0 a pressure difference and 1 a volumetric flow; `m0` as `mAir0`; `delay`, the delay of the opening (I-60). An outside pressure other than the ambient one has a note.

**I-32 Control valve.** `surgeControl: 'ideal'`. Its `surgeRate` is the smallest speed of the elements of `G4:rates` of each kind its flag applies (the closing ones with `rateClose`, the opening ones with `rateOpen`), with a note when they differ and one that asks for the characteristic of the valve, which the limit needs and the file does not give. Its options of the transient are read by their codes (answer 4.2), booleans −1 yes and 0 no: `always` −1 controls always and never fails, 0 may fail (the default); `defaultFail` −1 takes the actions on a failure of its kind (the default), 0 those of `failIn`, when the inlet side keeps it from controlling, and `failOut`, the outlet side, each 0 to open fully and stop controlling, so that it may control again, and 1 to close (a −1 of old files is 0); a pressure reducing valve opens and closes (0, 1), a sustaining one the reverse (1, 0), a flow control valve opens (0, 0), a pressure drop valve takes those written; `relFail` and `T:failTable` a manoeuvre after the failure over time (no rows: at once); `ovrFn` 0 no override, 1 a low inlet pressure, 2 a high outlet pressure, 3 a high flow. There is no note when `always` is 0, the actions in effect are those of its kind, `T:failTable` has no rows and `ovrFn` is 0; otherwise a note (I-61). Its set point over time comes in `j.surge`, as the quantity of its kind.

**Valves (section 14.5).** A Cv taken from a table of Cv by opening (`cvFromTable` 1) and an opening profile of the program (`profile` other than 0, data that are not in the file) have a note.

**I-33 Force sets.** The sets that `ForceSetsApplied` of `[FORCE SET CONTROL]` lists, along the chain of scenarios, in its order (`forceSets`): types 0 and 2 are `kind: 'segment'`, 1 `'point'`, 3 and 4 `'exit'` with `As` (in `ForceSetExitAreaUnits`); the positions along the pipes in `ForceSetLengthUnits`; `ambient` `'atm'`, or `'given'` with `pamb` when `ForceSetAmbientPressureUsage` is 1. A group or a unit vector filled (the vector is the last three fields of the line) has a note, and so has a type that is not known.

**I-34 Stored results of the junctions.** Their columns are not those of `.fth` files: 0 the ID, 1 the stagnation pressure (psia) and 3 the code of the kind of the junction. They are read as `storedJunctions` of the result of `read`, beside the stored results of the pipes, which are read as in `.fth` files (F-29) and compared by the report in the same way (F-31). A row whose kind is not that of the record of its junction is left out, with a note.

**I-35 Four quadrants in the steady state.** `fqRated` 2 takes the reference of the steady state, with a note that the steady state of the file, calculated with the four-quadrant curves, may not match the one of Pump It.

**I-36 Sinusoids.** `periodic` other than 0 on an imposed flow, an imposed pressure or a pump gets a note; a table can give the same manoeuvre.

## Decisions of the implementation

**I-37 Counts of a child record.** A count left empty in a child takes the count of the parent, and the child then holds the items of the parent, as −n does (section 12.3 of the description). A positive nY of a table is a new number of columns; another value keeps that of the parent. The count of the configurations of a pump is written after they are merged. Unlike the overlay of the fields of a `.fth` record, whose counts are not all known, a value of a list or a table is never taken for a count. A child record of another kind than its parent merges as in `.fth` files.

**I-38 Length of a record.** A record of a kind with a schema, of the base scenario or of a child, must end where its items do, or one field before (I-08). Otherwise the values after the first difference may be misread, and a note asks to check the junction.

**I-39 Cv or Kv of a generic component.** The K of the same loss at the area of the pipe that arrives at the component, by the relations of Pump It (a Cv of water at 999 kg/m³ in gpm and psi, a Kv of water at 1000 kg/m³ in m³/h and bar): K = 2·A²·psi/(999·gpm²·Cv²) and K = 200·(3600·A/Kv)². It is exact for a flow in the direction of the pipes; against it, Pump It refers the same K to the area of the other pipe, so the loss differs when the two pipes differ. A note says so. A Cv or a Kv that is not given is left blank for the user.

**I-40 Positive displacement pump.** The description reads it from the type of the pump and its fields `pd…` filled. A field counts as filled when it holds a number above zero, in the items of the cylinder, the speed and the coefficients (`DISPLACEMENT` in `imp.js`). Such a pump comes in by the mode of the file, as a `.fth` pump does (F-25), with a note that its model is out of scope.

**I-41 Version.** The description covers version 10 of the format. A file of another version is read as version 10, with a note.

**I-42 Keys of the fluid.** As section 10 of the description asks, an ambient pressure that varies with the elevation (`Ambient Pressure Variation` other than 0) and a gravity that varies (`VariableGExists` other than 0) are reported; Pump It keeps the ambient pressure and the gravity of the file.

**I-43 Validation on the user's files.** `node tests/validate-imp.js <folder>` imports every scenario with a network of every `.imp` file of a folder and its subfolders, checks that every pipe connects junctions of the model and that every number of the model is finite, and calculates the steady state of each scenario with stored results, comparing it with them by the measure of the report (section 19.3 of the description). The files stay on the user's computer: the script writes nothing.

**I-44 Units of the transient block of a child.** The check of I-10 takes "None" as an empty unit at w−4, w−6 and w−9, the units of the material a pipe may leave without a value, but not at w, the anchor itself.

**I-45 Trigger of a trip or a start.** In the program a pump with a trip (`tModel` 2, 3) or a start (4 to 6) changes its state only at an event of its trigger (answer 1). By the clock (`trig` 0) no event is tested: the program makes neither, and the pump keeps the state of its steady state through the run, as without transient data; so it comes in as `tModel` 0, without the trip, the start, the four-quadrant data or the table of the motor, with a note. One event (1) makes the trip or the start. Of two events (3 in sequence, 2 cyclic) the first makes it and the second the manoeuvre back, once (3) or alternating with the first (2): a restart after a trip, a trip after a start. Pump It makes one manoeuvre per pump (W-50): the first comes in, and the second is left out with a note that names it. A start with a known speed (4) takes its table of the speed at the first event, as one event. A trigger of another code leaves the data out, with a note.

**I-46 List of stations.** Station numbers from 0 at the inlet to N, the sections of the file (w+2), at the outlet, as Pump It numbers its stations (answer 2, confirmed on the files of the specification team). The numbers are those of the sectioning of the file: when Pump It, with the time step of the file, divides the pipe into n sections other than N (the check of I-14), station k becomes round(k·n/N), a half rounded up as the sectioning rounds, with a note. With the automatic search (no time step in the file, which then sets up no transient) n is not known at the import, and the list stays as the file gives it.

**I-47 A table cut at the start of the run.** A table by the clock in a run that starts after 0 keeps its rows from the start on, its value at the start taken on the straight line between the rows around it; a table that ends before the start keeps its last value.

**I-48 A pump whose state does not take its data.** A table of the speed or a trip needs a pump running on its curve, a start one stopped and closed (W-50). When the pump comes in otherwise (a control by the speed, a fixed flow, running where it should start), its data still come in, with a note, and the checklist of the water hammer shows the item until the user changes the pump.

**I-49 Speeds of the table of a motor.** The first column of the table of torque of a start is the speed on the axis of `startSpeedType` (answer 3), turned into % of the rated speed (I-26), with a note, since no file of the specification team has a start by the motor. The unit of the speeds confirms the axis (`RPM` with the type 0 only, `Percent` with 1 and 2): a unit that does not match is read on the axis of `startSpeedType`, with a note. Without the rated speed of the pump (types 0 and 2) or the synchronous speed of the motor (type 2), or with another code, the table is left out and a note asks for it.

**I-50 Pipes created by the import.** The pipe of 1 mm that the reader of the network adds for a pump on a single pipe (F-25) has no transient block: it takes the wave speed of the other pipe of the pump, with a note.

**I-51 Milliseconds.** The unit of time of section 17 of the description is added to the table of times of `shared.js`, which the `.fth` and `.aro` importers also read: a time in milliseconds in their files, until then left out with a note, now comes in. None of the imports of their tests changes.

**I-52 Devices without the module.** The readers of the devices (`types` of `imp-surge.js`, given to the reader of the network by `imp.js`) return nothing without the module of the water hammer: a device then comes in as the `.fth` importer brings in the kinds it does not read (F-26), as in phase I1. A device closed by its special condition is a node of its pipes, without the device, with a note.

**I-53 Orifice and riser of a tank or an accumulator.** `restrictor` says whether there is an orifice, −1 yes and 0 no, the convention of `riser`; −1 in `CdIn` and `CdOut` is an empty value (answer 5). With 0 the coefficients and the area are not read, without a note. With −1 the orifice needs a discharge coefficient above zero each way and its area: `CdAin` = `CdIn`·A for the liquid into the device and `CdAout` = `CdOut`·A for the liquid out of it (W-41). There is no orifice of one way in the program, so a coefficient that is not above zero, or no area, leaves it out with a note. A riser (`riser` other than 0) takes its length, its diameter (or the diameter of its area `riserA`) and its friction factor (0.015, the default of Pump It, when the file gives none).

**I-54 Weight and liquid of a disc.** The program takes the weight of a disc as given and works out no buoyancy; the mass of liquid of `fluidMassType` and `fluidMass` only adds inertia (answer 6). A swinging disc: `md` = Wd/g with `fluidMass: 'none'`, so that its torque is Wd·rcg·cos(θ − φ) as in the program, and the liquid it drags adds 1.25·m_f·r² to its inertia, r = √(Ad/π) (the inertia of a disc of mass m_f about its edge), m_f by `fluidMassType`: 0 the liquid of the sphere of radius r, (4/3)·π·r³·ρ with the density of the file; 1 `fluidMass` in `fluidMassU`; 2 none. Without the area of the disc that inertia is not added, with a note. A sliding disc: Pump It applies m·d²x/dt² = … − (m − m_f)·g·sin φ, so m = WdT/g + m_a and m_f = m_a (`fluidMass: 'user'`) make its weight WdT·sin φ and its mass WdT/g + m_a, those of the program; m_a is the mass given (1) or none (2), and without one `fluidMass: 'none'` gives the same equation. Code 0 is a mass the program estimates at each step, ρ·`AdT`·|Δx|, with no counterpart in Pump It: none, with a note. In the totals, each mass and inertia is converted to SI and the sum given in the unit of the disc.

**I-55 Spring of a hinge and of a sliding disc.** A unit is there once the merge with the parent is done (section 12.3): `None`, `(None)`, `NA`, `(User Specified)` and the empty field are not a unit (second answers, "how to read"). `ksU` is always a unit of torque followed by `/deg`; there is no spring per radian, nor one in force per length, on a swinging disc (answer 7.1). `N-m/deg`, `lbf-ft/deg`, `lbf-in/deg` and `kg-m/deg` are read as N·m per degree of Pump It, whose unit is N·m/rad: k_s = `ks`·(the torque in N·m)·180/π; another unit has a note asking for it. There is no spring when `ksU` is not a unit (whatever `ks` is, −999 too) or `ks` is 0: the program then applies no torque of a spring, nor `Ts0`, in the movement, in the steady state or in the reopening threshold, so neither comes in. `Ts0` acts only with `ks` above 0. The units of `ks` and `Ts0` go together: with only one of them the program refuses the valve and the model does not run (a note; the spring is read as the file has it). A `ks` negative with its unit is invalid data: no spring, with a note. A sliding disc (second answers, 1): k = `ksT` in the units of springs of section 17 only with its unit and `ksT` above 0, and F_s0 = `Fs0` only with that k and the unit of `Fs0`; otherwise k = 0 and F_s0 = 0. `ksT` 0 with `Fs0` not 0: the program still reopens the closed disc above (WdT·sin φ + `Fs0`)/AdT, Pump It, with no separate threshold, above WdT·sin φ/AdT (F_s0 = 0 keeps the movement, which decides the closing and the peak), and a note gives both. A `ksT` negative with its unit: no spring, with a note. The program refuses a sliding disc without the unit of `xMax`, `AdT`, `WdT`, `ksT` or `Fs0`, with `fluidMassType` 1 without that of `fluidMass`, or with fewer than 2 rows in `T:cvTravel`: a note, and the disc is read as the file has it.

**I-56 Table of the Cv of a disc.** `T:cvTheta` starts at `thMin` with the Cv fully open and ends at `thMax` with Cv 0: from the seat its positions are `thMax` − angle. `T:cvTravel` is on the travel of the program, measured from its stop fully open: it starts at 0 with the Cv fully open and ends at `xMax` with Cv 0, so the travel from the seat, that of Pump It, is `xMax` − x (answer 7, a correction of the reading before it, in which a sliding disc came in reversed). The positions, in degrees or in the unit of `xMax`, are sorted and cut at the seat and at the stop on the straight lines of the table (its end values held beyond it); its values are divided by the value at the stop, × 100. A table without a Cv above zero at the stop has a note, and the checklist of the water hammer asks for it.

**I-57 Position, seat and counterweight of a swinging disc.** The program measures θ between the arm of the disc (from the hinge to its centre of gravity) and the axis of the pipe, `thMax` closed and `thMin` fully open, 0 ≤ `thMin` < `thMax` ≤ 90°, and closes the disc by the torque Wd·rcg·cos(θ − φ) + cwW·cwL·cos(θ − φ − δ) + Ts0 + ks·(thMax − θ) − ΔP·Ad·rcg (answer 7). With θ_P = `thMax` − θ, the seat γ_c = 90° − `thMax` and δ = `cwAngle`, θ_P + γ_c + φ = 90° − θ + φ, so the torques of the weight, of the counterweight and of the spring of Pump It (G3-05) are those of the program. `orient` 0 is horizontal (φ = 0), 1 vertical with the flow upwards (`'vertical'`, φ = 90°), 2 the angle `orientAngle` in degrees, positive with the flow rising (`'angle'`, `phi`); another code keeps it horizontal, with a note; the same for a sliding disc, whose weight closes it with φ positive. The counterweight exists when at least one of the units of `cwL`, `cwW` and `cwI` is there, not by its angle (second answers, 2; −181 is the angle of a new valve, −999 that of an empty record, neither a sign): each of the three is read with its own unit and is zero without it, whatever its value, and `cwI` adds to the inertia of the disc even without `cwW` and `cwL`; its weight `cwW`, its arm `cwL` and its angle `cwAngle` are `Wc`, `rc` and `delta`. With a unit there, the program refuses the valve (and the model does not run) when one of the three values is negative, counted without a unit too, −999 included, or `cwAngle` is outside −180° to 180°: nothing of the counterweight is read then, torque or inertia, with a note saying why. The counterweight is out of the pressure at which the program reopens the closed disc, (Wd·rcg·cos(`thMax` − φ) + `Ts0`, only with `ks` above 0)/(rcg·Ad), which Pump It cannot hold apart from its torques: with a counterweight that has a torque, a note gives that threshold. The cases T26 and T27 of the addendum of phase G3, written as `.imp` files in the convention of the program (the angles of the arm, the weight without buoyancy, the travel from the stop fully open, the mass of liquid of a sliding disc), give its results to their tolerances in `tests/imp-import.test.cjs`.

**I-58 Window of the deceleration.** `decelWindow` is a time in seconds, without a field of unit (answer 8): above 0 it is `j.decelWindow`. The program takes then the difference between the oldest and the newest velocities of the last max(2, round(`decelWindow`/Δt)) steps with a forward flow, and Pump It the slope of a straight line by least squares over max(2, round(τ_d/Δt) + 1) steps (G3-04), the same for a linear fall. −1 (or 0) is another method of the program, the average deceleration since the velocity fell below that of the steady state, which Pump It does not have: its window of 0.1 s stays, with a note. `NumDecelPoints` of the control of the run is kept by the program and not read by its check valves, so it is not read either.

**I-59 Base of the pressures of a relief valve.** `crack`, `over` and `blowdown` are always on one base (answer 10.1): `crackType` 0 heads of the liquid (a unit of length), 1 pressures; `balanced` 0 differences over the outlet pressure `back`, −1 the pressures at the valve. `over` and `blowdown` are never relative to `crack`. With `balanced` 0 any unit of pressure is read as a difference (10 bar is a difference of 10 bar, an absolute or a gauge unit taken without its reference) and a head by ρ·g·h, and the pressure is that of the outlet plus the difference, given as a gauge pressure in the scale of the unit (psig for psid, bar(g) for bar, kPa(g) for a head in metres). With `balanced` −1 a pressure is absolute or gauge by its unit, as the other pressures of the file, and a head is a piezometric head, the pressure at the elevation of the valve with the ambient pressure, P_amb + ρ·g·(H − z). A `crackType` of another code takes the category of the unit. A unit of the other category, or a unit of difference with `balanced` −1, has a note.

**I-60 Air valve.** Its opening pressure `crack` is a pressure, absolute or gauge by its unit (`crackType` 1), or a piezometric head (0), the pressure at the elevation of the valve as for a relief valve (answer 10.2). The air in the valve is at the temperature of the liquid (isothermal): `liqTType` 0 that of the fluid of the model, the temperature of the fluid data of the file, 1 `liqT` in `liqTU`, as `Tair`. `gasText` and `gasPext` are the outside air, which the program takes for the air that comes in, and Pump It at the temperature of the valve too: an outside temperature other than that of the valve has a note. `gasType` 0 is air and 1 another gas with `molWt` and `gamma`; the program always takes the constant of the gas from `molWt` and keeps those of air with `gasType` 0, so the gas is taken for air unless its molar mass or its ratio of specific heats differ from those of air (W-22). The switch of the intermediate stage goes by `trigType`, not by the unit (answer 10.3): 0 a pressure difference over the outside pressure, in any unit of pressure, `switchBy: 'dP'` with `switchDP` = `trig`, the same sense in both; 1 the volumetric flow of the air expelled, at the outside pressure and the temperature of the valve, which the program keeps the intermediate stage below, while Pump It switches to it once the mass flow expelled passes its limit: `switchBy: 'flow'` with `switchFlow` = `trig`·ρ_air, ρ_air = `gasPext`/(R·T), R = 8314.462618/M J/(kg·K) with M = `molWt` (air without it), and a note that the switch acts the other way (a mass flow in the file is taken as it is). A switch that does not read keeps the default of Pump It, with a note. `delay` is the delay of its opening, in seconds, which Pump It does not have: a note.

**I-61 Failure of a control valve.** Answer 4.2 leaves to this implementation whether the ideal control valve of Pump It covers the actions on a failure. A control valve comes in as the `.fth` importer brings it (F-20): not always controlling, failing fully open. The control valve of Pump It then fails as its kind does, in the steady state and in the ideal transient: a pressure reducing valve opens fully when its inlet pressure is too low and closes against a reverse flow, a sustaining one closes when its inlet side keeps it from controlling and opens fully when the outlet side does, a flow control valve opens fully. These are the actions of each kind in the program, so a valve with them has no note, and any other choice has one (I-32). The option `always` (−1) is not taken as "always control" of Pump It, which would also change its steady state. A pressure drop valve of Pump It never fails; the program takes the actions written for it, which give no note.
